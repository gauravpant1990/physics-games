e = SEPARATION / APPROACH

A basketball dropped from 1.8 m hits the court at 6 m/s. The court returns e times the speed. Read the rebound height.
SET e, THEN PLAY
e IS A SPEED RATIO, NOT AN ENERGY RATIO

The coefficient of restitution is defined along the line of impact: e = speed of separation / speed of approach. A fixed floor has zero velocity throughout, so for a bounce the ratio is simply e = vrebound/vimpact.

Heights follow from v² = 2gh. Impact speed is √(2gh), rebound speed is e√(2gh), so the rebound height is h₁ = e²h and, inverted, e = √(h₁/h). From 1.8 m to 1.25 m: e = √(125/180) = √(25/36) = 5/6.

Set e = 1: the ball returns to exactly h. That is the perfectly elastic case, kinetic energy fully recovered. Any e < 1 loses KE in the impact, so momentum conservation alone can never guarantee an equal rebound.
