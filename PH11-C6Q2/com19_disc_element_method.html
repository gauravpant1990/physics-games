THE RADIUS COMES FROM PYTHAGORAS

A disc at height y has its centre on the axis. Its rim lies on the sphere, so the disc radius a, the height y and R form a right angle triangle: a² + y² = R², giving a = √(R² − y²).

Its volume is area times thickness, so dm = ρπ(R² − y²)dy.

The range is 0 to R, not −R to R, because a hemisphere occupies only the upper half. Note also that the radius is not R, which would be a cylinder, and not y, which is the height.
